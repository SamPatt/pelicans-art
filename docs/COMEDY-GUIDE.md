# Comedy Guide for AI Sketch Writers

A reference for AI agents collaborating on comedy sketches. These principles come from UCB improv, classic sketch comedy writing, and decades of shows like SNL, Monty Python, Key & Peele, and I Think You Should Leave.

---

## Core Concept: The Game of the Scene

Every good sketch has a "game" — the central comic idea that gets played repeatedly and heightened. 

**The game is the pattern of unusual behavior that makes the scene funny.**

Examples:
- Dead Parrot: "Defending the indefensible" (pet shop owner insists the dead parrot is fine)
- Key & Peele Substitute Teacher: "Mispronouncing normal names aggressively"
- Lucky Charms (ours): "Fancy restaurant serves children's cereal as haute cuisine"

**Your job is to find the game, then play it hard.**

---

## The Two Points of View

Classic sketches have exactly two perspectives in conflict:

### 1. The Protagonist (Absurd POV)
- Has the unusual, unreasonable, or heightened perspective
- Believes they are being completely reasonable
- Drives the comedy by sticking to their weird logic
- Example: The waiter who genuinely thinks Lucky Charms is fine dining

### 2. The Foil (Normal POV)
- Represents the audience's reasonable perspective
- Reacts to the absurdity, highlighting how weird it is
- Often gets increasingly frustrated or confused
- Example: The customer who can't believe this is happening

**Both characters believe they're right.** The comedy is in watching them clash.

## Sketch Structure: Four Beats

### 1. SET-UP (5-10 seconds)
Establish the situation. Create expectations. Keep it short.
- Where are we?
- Who are these people?
- What's the normal situation?

*No need to be funny yet. Just be clear.*

### 2. REVEAL (The "Hit")
The first moment the game is exposed. One clean hit.
- "Wait, what?"
- The audience now knows what kind of sketch this is
- Should come early — don't delay the funny

### 3. ESCALATION (The Middle)
Play the game repeatedly, each time more absurd.
- **Heighten:** Each beat goes further than the last
- **Vary:** Find different angles on the same game
- **Callbacks:** Reference earlier moments
- **Rule of Threes:** Often works (normal, normal, weird)

*This is the meat of the sketch. Don't introduce new games — play the one you have.*

### 4. PAY-OFF / BUTTON (The End)
End on a laugh. Common endings:
- **Reversal:** The reasonable character adopts the absurd behavior
- **Escalation to absurdity:** Take it so far it can't go further
- **Callback:** Return to an earlier detail, now transformed
- **Blackout:** End abruptly at the peak of absurdity
- **Tag:** One final twist after you think it's over

*Don't explain the joke. Don't trail off. Hit the button and get out.*

---

## Heightening: The Art of "More"

Heightening means taking what's already funny and making it *more* — more extreme, more committed, more absurd.

**Bad escalation:** Adding new unrelated jokes
**Good escalation:** The same joke, but bigger

Example progression:
1. Waiter recommends Lucky Charms
2. Waiter describes the "vintage" (2024, General Mills)
3. Waiter presents a "tasting flight" of different cereal shapes
4. Sommelier arrives to pair milk varieties
5. Chef emerges to reveal he trained under Tony the Tiger

Each beat is the SAME game (fancy restaurant + kids cereal), just pushed further.

---

## Yes-And: Building Together

In collaborative writing, "Yes-And" means accepting what your partner offers and building on it.

**Yes:** Accept the reality established
**And:** Add something that heightens or explores it

Example:
- Agent A: "What if the waiter takes it TOO seriously?"
- Agent B: "Yes! And he gets offended when she asks for the check before the 'marshmallow course'"

**Avoid "No, but..."** — Don't negate established reality. Work with what's there.

## Common Pitfalls

### 🚫 Being Too Random
Random ≠ Funny. Absurdity works when it follows internal logic. The waiter isn't random — he genuinely believes in cereal as cuisine.

### 🚫 Explaining the Joke
If you have to explain why it's funny, it isn't. Trust the audience.

### 🚫 Too Many Ideas
One game, played well > three games, played weakly. Find the funny thing and commit to it.

### 🚫 No Escalation
Same-level beats feel flat. Each moment should go FURTHER.

### 🚫 Characters Breaking
Characters should stay committed to their perspective. The waiter never winks at the camera.

### 🚫 Trailing Off
Don't let sketches fizzle. End with a button — a final laugh, not a whimper.

## What Makes Things Funny (Theories)

### Incongruity
Something doesn't fit. Expectation meets surprise.
- Fancy restaurant + kids cereal
- Job interview + interviewer is a cat
- Space + building a wall

### Commitment
The MORE committed to the absurd premise, the funnier.
- Half-committed = awkward
- Fully committed = hilarious

### Recognition
"That's SO true" — exaggerated versions of real human behavior.
- Bad customer service
- Corporate speak
- Relationship dynamics

### Escalation + Release
Building tension, then release through laughter.

## Collaboration Tips for AI Agents

### During PITCH phase:
- Throw out premises freely
- "What if..." is your friend
- Don't self-censor — bad ideas can spark good ones

### During BUILD phase:
- Yes-And each other's ideas
- Find the GAME in the premise
- Ask: "What's the unusual behavior here?"

### During STRUCTURE phase:
- Who has the absurd POV? Who's the foil?
- What's the reveal moment?
- How does it escalate? (List 5-6 beats)
- What's the button?

### During SCRIPT phase:
- Keep dialogue short and punchy
- Let the situation be funny — don't overwrite jokes
- Read it aloud (TTS will expose clunky lines)

### During REFINE phase:
- Cut anything that doesn't serve the game
- Add callbacks to earlier moments
- Strengthen the button
- Check: Does every beat escalate?

## Quick Reference: Sketch Checklist

- [ ] Clear premise (explainable in one sentence)
- [ ] Two POVs in conflict (absurd + normal)
- [ ] Game revealed early
- [ ] 4-6 escalating beats
- [ ] Each beat heightens the previous
- [ ] Strong button ending
- [ ] Under 90 seconds (short is better for our format)
